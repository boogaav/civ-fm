How the human world works.

If you arrived from outside, an alien or a new mind, this is the order to learn us in. Seven layers, each resting on the one below. Every layer is a station on the CIV FM dial, and every number has a source.

  1. 1EarthThe planet. Can I breathe, is the ground safe, can I drink?
  2. 2BodyThe species. Will this place keep me healthy, and what may I legally consume?
  3. 3HomeThe settlement. What is it like where you actually live, not the country average?
  4. 4MoneyThe exchange. What does a life cost here, and what is my money worth?
  5. 5StateThe rules. Who governs here, by what rules, and when do people vote?
  6. 6MindThe meaning. What do people here believe, read and listen to, right now?
  7. 7FutureThe trajectory. What will this place become in the worst-case century?

Layer 1 · 88.0 FM · The planet

Earth

Everything else stands on this: air to breathe, ground that holds, water to drink.

“Can I breathe, is the ground safe, can I drink?”

Air
Each dot is the temperature in a major city at this moment. The shaded half of the globe is night. Click anywhere for local weather, wind, UV and air quality.Source: Open-Meteo weather and air-quality models. City temperatures refresh every 10 minutes.
Ground
Earthquakes of the last 24 hours, plus storms, wildfires, volcanoes and floods that are active now.Source: USGS earthquake feed (magnitude 2.5 and up), NASA EONET open natural events, and GDACS, the UN and EU disaster alert system. Refreshed every few minutes.
Water
Water stress: the share of a country's renewable freshwater that people withdraw each year. Above 25% is stressed. Above 100% means taking more than nature puts back.Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator ER.H2O.FWST.ZS, latest year each country reported. Originally from FAO AQUASTAT. Local rain, soil moisture and river flow come from Open-Meteo.

Layer 2 · 91.0 FM · The species

Body

On that planet live eight billion bodies. How long they last, who heals them, what they put into themselves.

“Will this place keep me healthy, and what may I legally consume?”

Life
Life expectancy at birth, in years. Green is longer. The panel adds doctors per 1,000 people and health spending.Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator SP.DYN.LE00.IN, latest year each country reported.
Care
Doctors per 1,000 people in each country. Green means more physicians to go round. The panel adds health spending as a share of GDP.Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator SH.MED.PHYS.ZS, latest year each country reported. Originally from the WHO.
Cannabis
Whether cannabis is legal for adults, medical only, decriminalised or tolerated, illegal, or illegal with severe penalties.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national laws, snapshot of September 2026. Laws change, so check locally before you rely on it.
Alcohol
Whether alcohol is legal, restricted through permits or dry regions, or banned outright.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national laws, snapshot of September 2026. Laws change, so check locally before you rely on it.
Tobacco
Rules for tobacco and nicotine vapes: both legal, vapes prescription-only or tobacco sales restricted, or vapes banned.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national laws, snapshot of September 2026. Laws change, so check locally before you rely on it.
Psychedelics
Legal status of psilocybin mushrooms and other natural psychedelics, from legal or unregulated to illegal with severe penalties.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national laws, snapshot of September 2026. Laws change, so check locally before you rely on it.

Layer 3 · 93.0 FM · The settlement

Home

Bodies cluster into places. A country average hides the region a person actually lives in.

“What is it like where you actually live, not the country average?”

Living
The Human Development Index for each region: health, schooling and income combined into one number from 0 to 1. Above 0.8 is very high, below 0.55 is low.Source: Global Data Lab, Subnational HDI, 2019.
Health
Life expectancy at birth in each region, in years. Green is longer.Source: Global Data Lab, Subnational HDI, 2019.
School
Average years of schooling completed by adults aged 25 and over in each region. The panel adds how many years today's children can expect.Source: Global Data Lab, Subnational HDI, 2019.
Income
Gross national income per person in each region, adjusted for local prices. The scale is logarithmic: each step is ten times more.Source: Global Data Lab, Subnational HDI, 2019, in 2011 international dollars.
Internet
Measured download speed on fixed lines in each region, in megabits per second, from real speed tests. The panel adds mobile.Source: Ookla Open Data, Speedtest results aggregated to 600 m tiles, latest quarter, averaged per region weighted by the number of tests.
Lights
Earth at night. The lights show where electricity reaches people. The panel gives the share of people with electricity and with internet, and the measured internet speed in this region.Source: NASA Black Marble, a cloud-free composite from the VIIRS satellite sensor (2016), served by NASA GIBS. Access figures: World Bank, indicators EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS and IT.NET.USER.ZS.

Layer 4 · 98.0 FM · The exchange

Money

Settled people trade. Money is how they keep score of work, debt and value.

“What does a life cost here, and what is my money worth?”

GDP
Economic output per person per year, adjusted for local prices (purchasing power parity), so a dollar buys the same in every country.Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.CD, latest year each country reported.
Inflation
How much consumer prices rose over one year. Green is stable. Red means money is losing value fast.Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG, latest year each country reported.
Gold
The dollar value of the gold held by a country's central bank. The scale is logarithmic: each step is ten times more.Source: World Bank Open Data: total reserves minus reserves without gold (FI.RES.TOTL.CD and FI.RES.XGLD.CD), latest year reported.
Debt
Central government debt as a share of what the economy produces in a year. Many countries do not report it and stay blank.Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator GC.DOD.TOTL.GD.ZS, latest year each country reported.
Income tax
The top personal income tax rate: what the highest slice of a salary is taxed at, before deductions and treaties.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national tax codes, snapshot of September 2026. Planning summary, not tax advice.
Corporate tax
The headline corporate income tax rate on company profits, combined where regions add their own.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national tax codes, snapshot of September 2026. Planning summary, not tax advice.

Layer 5 · 100.5 FM · The rules

State

Trade needs rules, so people agree on states: who governs, who votes, who may cross a border, who pays.

“Who governs here, by what rules, and when do people vote?”

System
How each country is governed: republic or monarchy, who holds power and how they got it. Click a country for its heads of state and government, legislature, voting age and constitution, with the full text one tap away.Source: CIA World Factbook, public domain, updated through 2025. Constitution texts: Constitute Project (Comparative Constitutions Project), CC BY-NC, copied to civ.fm.
Vote
Days until each country's next national election. Red is within a month, yellow within half a year, grey more than a year away or unknown.Source: Wikidata, refreshed daily. Snap elections and dates not yet set will be missing.
Parties
How concentrated power is in each parliament: the share of seats held by the largest party. Green is a fragmented house, red a single party holding almost everything. Click a country for every party with its logo, seats and share, and which one leads the government.Source: Seats: CIA World Factbook (public domain) for most countries, else the lower house's English Wikipedia infobox. Logos and colours: Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons. Share is of the lower or single house, not of cabinet posts.
Power
How long the current leader has held power: green is a new leader, red is decades. Click a country for up to 100 people who hold power there, office holders and the richest citizens, each linking to Wikipedia.Source: Wikidata: people currently holding an office of the country (no end date, still living) and citizens with a declared net worth. The ranking weights the office first, then prominence. Wikidata is edited by volunteers; a stale entry can keep a former holder on the list.
Tax
How much each government collects as a share of its economy. Click a country for the total in dollars and per person, how much of it is tax, where the money comes from (income, VAT, social contributions, trade), what it spends on health, education, the military and interest, and its top income and corporate tax rates.Source: World Bank World Development Indicators, from IMF Government Finance Statistics, latest year reported; central government only, so federations read low. Health spending: WHO; education: UNESCO; military: SIPRI. Tax rates: CIV FM's curated table, 2026.
Passport
Where one passport can go: green visa-free, teal on arrival, yellow e-visa, orange a visa in advance, red no admission. Pick the passport in the panel; it starts with the country you are in.Source: Passport Index dataset (ilyankou, MIT), copied to civ.fm; visa rules change, so check before you fly.
Citizenship
How many years you must live in a country before you can apply for citizenship by the standard route. Green is three years or less, red twenty or more.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from national nationality laws, snapshot of September 2026. Spouses, descent and investment routes are usually faster.
Immigration
Who each country's immigration system is built to attract: skilled talent, investors, nomads and retirees, guest workers, diaspora, open door, family and humanitarian, or largely closed.Source: Compiled by hand for CIV FM from published policy, snapshot of September 2026. Dominant approach only; most countries run several routes.

Layer 6 · 103.0 FM · The meaning

Mind

Above the rules sit beliefs: what people worship, read, listen to and talk about.

“What do people here believe, read and listen to, right now?”

Faith
The largest religion in each country. Click one for the full breakdown as reported, grouped into families.Source: CIA World Factbook, public domain; each country's latest census or survey estimate, which can be years apart.
Reading
What each country read most on Wikipedia yesterday. Click a country for its top ten articles and what it is searching for now.Source: Wikimedia pageviews, top articles per country, for yesterday in UTC. Searches: Google Trends daily feed.
Radio
Real radio from every country. Brighter countries stream more stations. Click one to list its stations, then click a station to listen. Popular, Rising and News switch the list.Source: Radio Browser, a free community directory, queried live. Only stations with a secure stream are listed, since browsers refuse the rest.
Signal
Every flash is a person editing Wikipedia, as it happens. Also the International Space Station and the next rocket launch.Source: Wikimedia EventStreams (live), wheretheiss.at for the ISS, and Launch Library for launches.

Layer 7 · 106.5 FM · The trajectory

Future

All of it is moving. The last layer asks where a place is heading this century.

“What will this place become in the worst-case century?”

Outlook
The balance for each region: what warming gives it (longer seasons, new crops, less heating) minus what it takes (heat, drought, sea level). Green gains more. Red loses more.Source: CIV FM's own climate emulator: IPCC AR6 warming for the SSP5-8.5 pathway, applied to WorldClim 2.1 climate normals (1970 to 2000) for 4,595 Natural Earth regions. It shows direction and rough size. It is not a forecast. The weights are CIV FM's own, meant for comparing places.
Climate zone
The climate zone each region falls into in the chosen year: tropical, arid, temperate, continental or polar.Source: CIV FM's own climate emulator: IPCC AR6 warming for the SSP5-8.5 pathway, applied to WorldClim 2.1 climate normals (1970 to 2000) for 4,595 Natural Earth regions. It shows direction and rough size. It is not a forecast. Zones follow the Köppen-Geiger rules (Beck et al. 2018).
Crops
How well eight staple crops could grow compared with today. Green means better conditions. Red means worse.Source: CIV FM's own climate emulator: IPCC AR6 warming for the SSP5-8.5 pathway, applied to WorldClim 2.1 climate normals (1970 to 2000) for 4,595 Natural Earth regions. It shows direction and rough size. It is not a forecast. Crop limits from FAO EcoCrop. Soils and irrigation are not modelled.
Warming
How much hotter each region is than before industry (1850 to 1900), in degrees Celsius. Land and the Arctic warm faster than the global average.Source: CIV FM's own climate emulator: IPCC AR6 warming for the SSP5-8.5 pathway, applied to WorldClim 2.1 climate normals (1970 to 2000) for 4,595 Natural Earth regions. It shows direction and rough size. It is not a forecast.
